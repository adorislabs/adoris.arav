For Math, focusing on each page is a good starting point, but it's not the "Correct" way if you want to build a truly smart tutor.

a strict "Page 1, then Page 2" rule might actually hurt user's learning.
The Problem with "Page-by-Page"

In a Math textbook, a single concept often spans half a page (like the introduction to "Lakh" on Page 1) or three pages (like a long exercise).

    Arbitrary Breaks: A page might end right in the middle of an example. If the AI stops there and asks a "Why" question, the student hasn't seen the final answer yet.

    Exercise Bloat: Page 13 of your PDF is just a list of word problems (Albatross and Godwit flight distances). If you treat this as one "page," the AI might try to teach all 14 problems at once, which is overwhelming.

The Better Way: "Logical Unit" (Topic) Parsing

Instead of "Page-by-Page," you should be doing "Topic-by-Topic" parsing. Here is how you restructure your PDF processing:
1. Group by Headings, not Page Numbers

Looking at your PDF, the natural breaks are:

    Section 1.1: "A Lakh Varieties!" (Conceptual Intro)

    Section 1.2: "How much is one lakh?" (Pattern/Number sense)

    Section 1.3: "Place Value" (Technical skill)

Your Logic: Tell the LLM to read the entire PDF, but break it into Logical Units.
2. The "Sub-Topic" Gate

Instead of a "Next Page" button, use a "Next Concept" button.

    Unit 1: Understanding the scale of 100,000.

    Unit 2: Writing numbers in Indian vs. International systems.

    Unit 3: Practical word problems.

3. Handling Exercises (The "Skill Check")

When the PDF reaches a section like "Figure it Out" (found on Page 3), the AI should switch modes. It shouldn't "teach" Page 3; it should curate it.

    Current way: AI asks about everything on Page 3.

    Better way: AI picks one problem from the "Figure it Out" section to test the student. If he gets it right, he skips the rest of the page. If he gets it wrong, the AI gives him a second problem from the same page.

Ensure page numbers based on the pdf are mentioned in response when REQUIRED only.
